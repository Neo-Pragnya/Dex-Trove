# Frameworks

Each framework below: what it asks, how to fill it from the gathered material, and the slide type that renders it.
Use the ones that fit the person; a 25-slide deck with the right five frameworks beats 50 slides of everything.

## 1. The four-circle Ikigai → `venn` + four `cards`

Love · Good at · World needs · Paid for. Each pair is an overlap with a typical expression and a typical trap:

| Overlap | Circles | Write for the person |
|---|---|---|
| Passion | Love + Good at | expression · pitfall (too personal / niche) · correction |
| Mission | Love + Needs | expression · pitfall (unpaid over-giving) · correction |
| Profession | Good at + Paid | expression · pitfall (golden handcuffs) · correction |
| Vocation | Needs + Paid | expression · pitfall (mechanical, soulless) · correction |

The three-circle **lenses** carry the classic warnings (built in): missing need → "satisfaction, but feeling of
uselessness"; missing pay → "delight and fullness, but no wealth"; missing love → "comfortable, but feeling of
emptiness"; missing skill → "excitement and complacency, but sense of uncertainty". Add a personal note per lens: how
that trap shows up for *this* person.

The centre is a one-sentence Ikigai ("I build intelligent systems that help humans evolve") plus its own risk
(often over-expansion) and correction.

Honesty note worth putting on the slide: the four-circle diagram is a modern Western adaptation; in Japan ikigai is
broader and includes everyday sources of meaning, independent of income.

Deep-dive `cards` per circle: up to 10 items, each icon + title + one line of why; end each with a `summary` that names
the pattern ("I love bridging intelligence and transformation").

## 2. Six-dimension Ikigai → `petals`

Add **Evolution** (what experiences will grow me?) and **Regeneration** (does this restore or deplete people,
communities, nature?) to Love, Capability, Need, Sustainability. The point: no single job satisfies all six, so you
design an *ecosystem* of activities that together do. Only use scores if the person gave them.

## 3. Contribution lenses (optional chapter)

- **Impact ≠ reach** (`cards`): depth, potential restored, duration, multiplication, probability, counterfactual,
  unintended effects. "Scale is not depth; visibility is not value."
- **Two evolutions** (`compare`): what changes in the world vs who I become.
- **Will · knowledge · action** (`triad`): what moves me, how deeply I understand what I serve, what becomes embodied
  action. (Iccha-Jñāna-Kriyā in Sri Vidya; use the person's own vocabulary.)
- **Relief → empowerment** (`chain`): direct help → train practitioners → build capability systems → institutions
  beyond me.
- **Vocations as curricula** (`table`): each path's contribution, growth and temptation; highlight theirs.

## 4. The connecting principle → `table`

Interest → what attracts me → deeper principle (Intelligence, Knowledge, Transmission, Multiplication, Freedom…). The
summary names the single thread. This slide turns "too many interests" into one identity.

## 5. Archetype → `triad`

Exactly three recurring roles (Scholar · Healer · Builder) with a centre title (e.g. "Conscious Systems Architect")
and a one-line definition. Add the caution: integration must rest on genuine mastery, not breadth.

## 6. Vision, mission, values → `vmv`, `statement`, `trinity`

Writing rules:
- **Vision** = the world as it will be when the work succeeds; present tense, about others, no "I".
- **Mission** = what I do about it: verb + for whom + how + to what end. One or two sentences.
- **Pillars** = 3-5 strategic "how"s, each something that could have its own roadmap.
- **Values** = 5-7 words, each with a 3-6-word gloss that says how it changes behaviour ("Generativity: capacity, not
  dependence").
- **Identity** = archetype title + one sentence.
- **Three aspirations** (`statement`): outward purpose, inward practice, generational legacy.
- **Trinity** (`trinity`): Service ("what is changing for others?") · Consciousness ("who am I becoming?") ·
  Generativity ("what becomes possible without me?"), ringed by **Sustainability** ("can this life support my health,
  relationships, responsibilities and joy?").

## 7. Operating principles → `wheel`

3-8 principles shown as a wheel; each segment gets rows such as *meaning · in my life · for others · inner growth ·
in my institution · failure to watch for*. One example lens is **Pañcakṛtya**, the five divine functions from the
Lalita Sahasranama: Sṛṣṭi (create), Sthiti (sustain), Saṃhāra (dissolve), Tirodhāna (veil / not-knowing), Anugraha
(grace / freedom). Present it as a *contemplative application*, not an authoritative theological claim. Other lenses:
Stoic virtues, the Christian works of mercy, the Buddhist pāramitās, the person's company values.

Pair with a review practice (`questions`): one question per principle at the end of each meaningful period, plus "did I
become more capable of loving and serving without needing to possess the results?"

## 8. Ecosystem → `cycle`, `ecosystem`, `cards`, `layers`

- **Role loop** (`cycle`): how the person's roles feed each other (practitioner identifies → researcher investigates →
  technologist builds → teacher distributes → founder sustains → philanthropist widens → contemplative reflects).
- **Institutions / products** (`ecosystem`): a hub plus 3-8 parts and the flows between them (evidence, tools,
  trained people, feedback, revenue, free access). Label every flow with *what* travels.
- **Parts in detail** (`cards`): include one "boundary" card (what is evidence-based vs symbolic; what is free vs paid).
- **Who pays vs who benefits** (`layers` with an upward arrow): open commons (free) · supported access (sponsored) ·
  professional services (paid), with the paid layer funding the others.

## 9. Strategy → `chain`, `scores`, `cards`, `compare`

- **Sequencing** (`chain`): e.g. consulting → productised service → platform → institute. Cashflow first.
- **Scoring** (`scores`): 5-15 options × 5-8 criteria scored 1-10 *by the person*. Default lenses: love, skill,
  need, money, rarity, soul, effort-fit. Top 3 are highlighted; viewers can re-rank by any lens.
- **North star** (`cards`): the one bet that seeds the whole ecosystem, and what it gives (cashflow, foundation,
  audience, reusable IP, freedom…).
- **Life architectures** (`compare`): 2-3 whole-life paths with attraction and challenge; mark one `recommended` only
  if the material supports it, and end with the question that decides between them.

## 10. Roadmaps → `roadmap`

Use 2-4 of these, each with 3-6 phases (`when`, `title`, `goal`, `items`), `now` marking where they stand:
- **Venture stages**: 0-6 months cashflow, 6-18 productise, 18-36 ecosystem, 3-5 years expansion.
- **First 90 days**: 15-day blocks from niche to paid pilot to public launch.
- **Contribution path**: test the living idea (90 days) → repeatable model (year 1) → primary vocation (years 2-5)
  → steward, not centre (5+).
- **Generations**: direct service → capability transfer → institutional independence → cultural transformation.

Finish with **three layers of a life** (`layers`): central mastery (depth) · generative contribution (multiplication)
· contemplative and relational life (wholeness, not productivity).

## 11. Inner compass → `cards` (tone `warn`), `table`, `questions`, `compare`, `statement`

- **Pitfalls → remedies**: their own patterns, each with a concrete remedy.
- **Symbolic timing** (only if they use astrology or similar): a table labelled *symbolic, not deterministic*.
- **Soul-evolution experiences**: encountering suffering, confronting uncertainty, creating, sustaining, relinquishing
  control, with what each can teach if reflected on.
- **Signs of maturation**: things you notice, not measure (less defensive, helping without needing gratitude…).
- **Life A / Life B** (`compare`): the accomplished life vs the contemplative life; which is foundational?
- **"Make greatness less necessary"** (`statement`): mastery that creates the conditions where it's no longer needed.

## 12. Close → `cycle`, `questions`, `statement`, `closing`

Purpose as a process (encounter → study → insight → create → share → find limits → release), three contemplation
questions, one image (the gardener), and a closing cascade of the words their life integrates.
