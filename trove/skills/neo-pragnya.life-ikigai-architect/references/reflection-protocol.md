# Reflection protocol

How to gather enough true material for a life architecture, whether from documents or from a conversation.

## From documents (preferred when they exist)

Read everything the person gives you, end to end: purpose conversations, Ikigai notes, journals, CVs, business-idea
lists, astrology notes. Then extract into the buckets below, keeping **their exact phrases** in quotes so you can reuse
them on slides. Note for each item whether it was *stated* or *inferred*.

| Bucket | Look for |
|---|---|
| Love | What energises them unpaid; recurring fascinations; what they lose time in |
| Good at | Skills others ask them for; credentials; tools; rare combinations |
| World needs | Pains they notice; who suffers; what is missing or broken |
| Paid for | Offers, buyers, revenue models, what has already sold |
| Overlaps | Passion / mission / profession / vocation expressions, pitfalls and corrections |
| Deeper principle | The thread under the interests ("understanding life and increasing its capacity to flourish") |
| Archetype | 2-4 recurring roles (scholar, healer, builder, guardian, artist, connector…) |
| Purpose statements | Any sentence that sounds like a mission or Ikigai; keep the best verbatim |
| Values | What they refuse to compromise; how they want to treat people |
| Tradition / lens | A spiritual, philosophical or cultural frame they use, and how literally they hold it |
| Ecosystem | Ventures, institutions, platforms, communities they imagine, and how the parts connect |
| Options & scores | Idea lists, any scoring they did, paths they are choosing between |
| Sequencing | Timelines, first steps, cashflow plans, 90-day plans |
| Pitfalls | Self-described patterns (perfectionism, over-giving, too many ideas…) and remedies |
| Inner life | What growth they want, what they fear, how they review themselves |
| Open questions | Decisions not yet made (e.g. "should I go to medical school?") |

Conflicts between documents are useful: surface them on a `compare` or `questions` slide rather than picking a side.

## From conversation (when material is thin)

Ask in **rounds of 3-5 questions**, reflect back what you heard in two or three sentences, then continue. Stop early if
the material is already rich. Skip rounds that don't apply.

**Round 1: the spark**
- What makes you feel alive even when no one is paying you?
- What could you talk about for hours? What do people say lights you up?
- When you were 12, what did you do without being told?

**Round 2: mastery**
- What have you spent years refining that others already seek your advice for?
- Which combination of your skills is unusual in your field?
- What do you find easy that others find hard?

**Round 3: the world**
- What pain in the world can your skills relieve? Who, specifically, suffers from it?
- What makes you angry or heartbroken when you see it?
- Whose lives would be different in ten years if you did this well?

**Round 4: the economics**
- What will people, companies or communities pay you for repeatedly? What has already sold?
- Who could pay so that someone else can receive it for free?
- What is your minimum monthly need, and your runway?

**Round 5: identity and the deeper thread**
- Across everything you've told me, what is the one thread you'd defend?
- Which 2-3 roles keep recurring in your life (e.g. scholar, healer, builder)?
- Do you hold a tradition, philosophy or symbol system that shapes how you see purpose? How literally?

**Round 6: options and order**
- What are the 5-12 ideas or paths competing for your energy?
- Score each 1-10 on: love, skill, need, money, rarity, soul, effort-fit (or your own criteria).
- What could you start in the next 90 days with what you already have?

**Round 7: the shadow and the inner life**
- What patterns tend to derail you? What has worked as a remedy?
- What kind of difficulty would you willingly endure because the work matters?
- If every title and achievement were taken away, what experience would you still want in this life?

## Then

1. Write a one-paragraph summary back to the person: their Ikigai sentence (draft), archetype, vision, mission and
   the top 3 bets. Ask what's wrong with it. Corrections here are cheaper than after the deck is built.
2. Offer 2-3 alternative wordings for the vision and mission; let them choose or blend.
3. Only then write the spec.
